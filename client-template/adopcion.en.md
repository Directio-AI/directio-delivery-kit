# Adoption: <client>

AI tools are powerful but overwhelming. Our job is to make them simple for the client's people: one assistant per role, one task at a time, in the tool they already have.

## Before training anyone

- [ ] The owner or management states clearly, in front of the team, what AI is for. Before that, in private, we ask whether the real aim is to cut headcount: if it is, we know before asking anyone for their trust.
- [ ] An internal champion chosen: curious, respected, with protected time (1 hour per week).
- [ ] One assistant configured per role in the client's account, with instructions and documents already loaded. Nobody starts with an empty chat.

## First session script (60 minutes, per role, at most 8 people)

| Minute | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | The sponsor says what this is for and what it is not for. |
| 5–15 | Each person writes down the task they hate most in their week. |
| 15–40 | We pick one of those tasks and do it live, with a real case from someone in the group, in the role's assistant. The case owner reviews the output and decides whether it is usable. |
| 40–55 | Everyone repeats with their own case. We help. |
| 55–60 | Commitment: 30 minutes this week on that task. Where to note what they saved. |

Session rules: real work, not toy examples; the person keeps the judgment and the credit; no new screens.

## "Empieza aquí" (start here) page, one per role

The page itself is written in Spanish for the client. Its structure:

> **Start here: <role>**
>
> **What this is for:** <one line from the sponsor>.
>
> **Your assistant:** <link to the role's project or GPT>. It already knows <documents loaded>.
>
> **Three things you can ask it today:**
> 1. <task 1, with an example of how to ask>
> 2. <task 2>
> 3. <task 3>
>
> **What it must not do:** <e.g. send anything to a customer without your review; receive card data or passwords>.
>
> **If something looks wrong:** write to <internal champion>.
>
> **Tell us what you saved:** <link to the form or channel>.

## Monthly follow-up

The team reports its wins (not us). A 30-minute meeting each month: what was saved, what did not work, which task is next.
